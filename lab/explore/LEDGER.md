## Notes for later runs (the lead, 2026-11-18)
- **GDELT 1.0 daily event files** are written at about 07:00 UTC on D+1 since 2015-03, between 07:34 and 14:15 UTC from
  2014-02 to 2015-02, and the days of 2013-04 to 2014-01 were back-processed in February 2014 (not as first
  published). A rule on these files enters at the 08:00 UTC bar of D+1 or later. Evidence:
  `ideas/002_news_tone/gdelt_last_modified.txt`.
- **Wikimedia page views** answer HTTP 429 to bursts from the cloud's shared address: one request every 10 seconds or
  slower. **CFTC (Socrata)** gives the whole history in one request. **FINRA short-volume files**: about 3,900 small
  files came through with 2 threads and pauses. **FRED and Yahoo** answer the kit's readers.
- **Closest miss on record: idea 025** (long the four indices from the Monday to the Friday of options-expiry week):
  PF 1.42 net, 7 of 9 years, null at the 99.6th percentile, correlation with the ten 0.00, but t on daily sums 1.65
  against 2.5 (the four indices are one bet) and both 2020 and 2021 negative. Closed; testing a variant of it on the
  same years would be tuning.
- **Kit, fixed on 2026-11-18 (kit v2):** hedged pairs, exits on a condition (which also express "exit on a given

  - **Slow trends give nothing before costs** (055 on 14 FP markets: PF gross 1.04; 058 on the 40 shares: below a
    random entry on the same share and side). Swaps then take 19 to 49 bp a trade.
  - **Earnings on the 40 shares:** buying before the expected date is the shares' own rise (053, null at the 48th
    percentile); following the move during the results day loses even gross (054). The five-session drift (052) has a
    gross edge (PF 1.31, +32 bp a trade) eaten by about 30 bp of costs. Read by size AFTER the verdict (a hypothesis,
    not a finding): nil below 5 %, +133 bp gross at 8 % or more (80 trades, t 2.5 gross, every year positive).

  - **The random-entry null alone says little** for a rule with event exits (056: 99.9th percentile with t 1.4) or

- **Batch of 2026-11-19 (ideas 021, 023, 042, 043, 059, 060, 062 and 063; kit v2.2; every figure re-run by the lead):**
  all eight fail.
  - **Direction 3 is closed.** 059 was the out-of-sample test of the megacaps' reading (follow a results-day move of
    8 % or more for five sessions, on 382 other shares): n 524, PF gross 1.11 (+16 bp a trade, -3 bp without 2020), PF
    net 0.71, 0 of 5 years. The +133 bp of the 40 megacaps was luck in a bucket chosen after the verdict. 060 (the same,
    held eight weeks): longs after up-shocks +319 bp gross (t 4.0), but on a list that holds only today's index members,
    in a rising market, with the 55 longs of 2020 giving two thirds of it; shorts lose before costs; PF net 0.86.
    062 (fade large moves without news on calm days): PF gross 1.11, unstable by year, PF net 0.80. The heavy
    provisional costs are not what killed these three (at a 12 bp open spread, information only, they still lose):
    the gross edges are too small. No further idea on US shares around results or news unless FP's real share costs
    are known AND a point-in-time universe exists.
  - **Crypto gauges.** 021 (Coinbase premium): t 1.37 is the only failed bar (null 98.4), but the profit is the long
    side (t 2.38) and the year 2019. 043 (Korean premium, short): PF 1.14, t 0.86, one episode (winter 2018). 023
    (absorbed order flow): nothing before costs. 042 (short bitcoin after dear-dollar days): PF 0.62, null at the 2nd
    percentile.
  - **The mirror trap (063).** 042's loss looked like a signal with the wrong sign. Its exact mirror was declared post
    hoc (the row of IDEAS.md was committed before any run, with one more validation bar) and tested: PF net 1.40, but
    t 2.25, null 91.3, and against random long entries of the SAME YEAR the 52nd percentile: no timing, only
    bitcoin's drift in the years the signals fall in (164 of 351 in 2017). 042's 2nd percentile was the same artefact
    upside down. No validation look was taken. Lesson: a large loss of a one-sided rule on a drifting market is not a
    mirror edge.
  - **The same-year null on today's crypto mains** (information, computed afterwards with kit v2.3; its judged null
    reproduces the judged figures exactly): 063 long 91.3 -> 43; 042 short 1.95 -> 58; 043 short 96.2 -> 85; 021
    (both sides, signals spread evenly over the years) 98.35 -> 97.1, its buying side alone 96.1 -> 96.4 (t 2.38),
    its selling side 84 -> 81. So 021's buying side is the one result of the day that is not the drift of its years;
    it is also the best side picked among eight ideas, which is selection. Idea 064 (reserved the same day, before
    any run) tests it on ether, never looked at, with two conditions written beforehand on any validation look.
  - **Kit v2.3 follows from it** (the same day; two independent reviews): the same-year null as information
    (`stats.null_test(within="year")`, `judge.verdict(null_year=)`): a permutation test inside each year (distinct
    days, long and short together, the same clock in New York and in UTC, inside the span the rule traded). The first
    version drew with replacement in the whole calendar year: it gave 063 the 76th percentile because 32 of its
    trades fall in the first two months of its data (the lead's cross-check against the run's own loop found it),
    and it was too wide for a rule that takes most days of a year (the verifier measured it). A calendar of labels
    made by `data.event_table` takes `row_len` 0 with a `market` column (059, 060 and 062 had to rename it); one with
    a numeric column does not (a number computed from a session's bars and stamped before its close looks ahead and
    no cut can see it: numbers belong inside build). Written down in the README: a 24-hour exit on a daily signal is
    taken one day in two (042 and 043 declared 23 hours); `data.session_moves` is slow on the whole universe (a
    truncation test takes 10 to 12 minutes). The judged null bar now also fails for a null that was not computed
    from the main (its mean R differs), `judge.val_verdict` judges the charter's extra bar of a one-sided rule
    (`"extra_null_bar": 90` in `val_declared.json`; a value that does not bind is an error), and `data.unlock_val`
    keeps the validation period locked for a one-sided main without it (the second review found that `null` or 0
    switched the bar off in silence).
  - **Process slip to avoid:** two agents (060, 062) ran their information-only cost variant before the judged run
    had finished (the declarations were already hashed and nothing was changed). From the next batch on the brief
    says the judged run comes first.
