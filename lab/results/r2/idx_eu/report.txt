# Track `idx_eu` — DAX CFD (ger40), round 2

## Verdict

**Nothing on this track survives convincingly.**

- **Opening-range breakouts, the reason this track existed, are not an edge on the DAX.** Under the real FP
  costs the round-1 rule (09:00–10:00 Berlin range) gives PF 1.08, t 1.3, and all of it is the long side
  (+81.5 R long, −12.5 R short). Seven ORB variants (cash open 15/30/60 min, pre-market range, US open):
  net PF 0.94–1.08, none with t above 1.3.
- **One in-sample effect looked real: momentum into the Xetra close, measured from the previous cash
  close.** Both finalists come from it.
  - `late_mom_1600` (IS PF 1.67, t 3.5) **failed validation**: PF 0.98, −0.008 R/trade on 151 trades.
  - `close_run_1725` (IS PF 1.50, t 5.1) **passes the letter of the validation criteria** (PF 1.105,
    +0.0105 R/trade on 618 trades). It is a weak pass: t 0.93, 2 of 4 years positive (2022 makes +13.0 R,
    the other three years −6.5 R together), and with costs ×1.5 it loses (PF 0.89). What is left is
    0.38 bp net per trade on a five-minute trade. I do not consider it confirmed and would not fund it
    before checking FP's own price history (section 6).
- 64 configurations evaluated (budget 120), every one logged. Two validation runs, one per finalist sleeve,
  decided beforehand in `finalists_preval.json`. No re-tuning afterwards.

## 1. Data and set-up

- Instrument: ger40 FP-cost frame, research data only (2026 never loaded). In-sample = entries before
  2022-01-01 (first bar 2013-09-30, so "2013" is three months).
- Data hours differ by year (Berlin clock): about 00:00–22:14 in 2014 and 2018–2021, only 08:00–22:00 in
  2015–2017, sparse in 2013. Every rule here uses 08:00–21:59 only.
- All clock times are Europe/Berlin wall clock (DST handled per date); the US open is taken as 09:30 New York.
- Engine: `r2.evaluate(full=True)` for every trial (FP costs, costs ×1.5 with orders rebuilt on the widened
  quotes, zero-cost run, every-signal run). All rules trade at most once a day and are flat the same day, so
  the every-signal run equals the base run.
- Causality: orders built on frames cut at random times and inside a trading day equal the orders of the
  full frame up to the cut: 15 cuts for each finalist's exact parameters, 9 cuts for each rule family
  (`causality_test.json`). A 4-month frame gives the same orders as the long frame once six weeks have
  passed (the scale needs 20 earlier days).
- us500 was read once, in-sample, as a control for the close run-up (section 2); it is not used by any rule.

### What the descriptive statistics say (IS)

- No tradable drift by time of day: every 30-minute bucket is within ±1.1 bp. Overnight (17:30→09:00) is
  +4.1 bp (t 2.4), the cash day −1.4 bp.
- No continuation between sessions in general (opening half-hour → rest of morning: +0.2 bp; US-open
  half-hour → rest of afternoon: +0.3 bp). No gap fade either (gap → first hour: −0.3 bp, t −0.3).
- Continuation exists only after a LARGE move from the previous cash close (|z| ≥ 1.5): to 17:30 it is
  +10 bp from 09:30 (t 1.8), +15 bp from 12:00 (t 3.0), +13 bp from 15:45 (t 4.7), +12 bp from 16:00
  (t 4.5), +9 bp from 16:30 (t 4.4). With the 09:00 open as anchor it is weak (16:00: +6 bp, t 2.0), and it
  does not last into the evening (16:00→21:55: +7 bp, t 1.9).
- Most of it sits in the last minutes. On big-move days 17:15→17:30 gives about +6 bp of the 12. On ALL days
  the trend-signed move is +0.55 bp over 17:25–17:28, +0.70 bp in 17:28–17:29 (t 6.7), +0.62 bp in
  17:29–17:30 (t 4.6), then −0.57 bp in the first minute after the close (t −5.0) and −0.84 bp over
  17:36–17:45. The 17:25→17:30 piece is positive in all nine IS years (0.8–3.1 bp).
- It is in the raw Dukascopy quotes too (buy at the ask 17:25, sell at the bid 17:30, days with |z| ≥ 0.5:
  +1.51 bp, t 4.1), it is inside the 17:28 and 17:29 bars and not a jump at the bar boundary, and the same
  provider's US500 CFD shows nothing similar before the New York close. So it is a price move in this
  feed, not a quoting artefact that I can identify. Whether FP's feed has it is not known.

## 3. Finalists (chosen on IS, written to `finalists_preval.json` before any validation access)

Both are family `mom` in `strategy.py`. Definitions shared by both, precise enough for an EA:

1. Clock: Europe/Berlin wall time (convert from the MT5 server clock, DST included).
2. P(t) = mid price (bid+ask)/2 at the close of the last M1 bar that started before t. If no bar started in
   the 5 minutes before t, or none starts in the 5 minutes after t, there is no trade that day.
3. A = P(17:30) of the most recent earlier weekday that was quoted at 17:30 (yesterday's cash close).
4. x = P(T)/A − 1. vol = sample standard deviation (n−1) of x over the 20 most recent earlier days on which
   x exists (today excluded; 20 values required).
5. If |x| ≥ k·vol: market order at T, long if x > 0, short if x < 0.
6. Stop-loss at stop_k·vol·P(T) from the fill price. No target, no trailing.
7. Market exit at 17:30:00. One trade per day.

| | `late_mom_1600` | `close_run_1725` |
|---|---|---|
| T | 16:00 | 17:25 |
| k | 1.5 | 0.5 |
| stop_k | 0.5 | 0.25 |
| holding time | 90 min | 5 min |

## 6. What would settle `close_run_1725`

Its doubt is not mainly statistical, it is whether FP's GER40 price shows the same last-minutes move.
MT5 keeps M1 history: on FP's own bars, take the sign of (17:24 close ÷ previous day's 17:29 close − 1) and
average the signed move from the 17:25 open to the 17:30 open on days where that move exceeds half its
20-day deviation. In this data it is +2.7 bp in 2013–2021 and roughly +1.3 bp in 2022–2025 (validation
result plus the modelled cost). The round trip costs about 1 bp: below that the sleeve loses, and the
in-sample performance needs the full 2.7 bp.

## 7. Engine and data concerns (nothing in `goldlab/` was changed)

1. **Cash-hours spread in the FP frame is 0.7× the nominal spread from 2018 on.** The intraday shape is
   Dukascopy's spread divided by that trading day's MEDIAN spread. From 2018 Dukascopy quotes the cash
   session tighter than the evening and the night, and the median minute of a 23-hour day is an evening
   minute, so the cash session gets a factor below 1 (clipped at 0.7). Modelled spread 09:00–17:29 Berlin:
   0.62 bp in 2013–2017, 0.436 bp in 2018–2025, against a nominal 1.5 points = 0.62 bp. The whole
   validation period is therefore charged about 1.05 points (at today's price) in cash hours. That is right
   only if FP's 1.5 points is an all-day figure and not its cash-hours spread; to be checked against the
   verified FP costs. For my two finalists the effect is small (about 0.09 bp per trade, entry side only:
   the 17:30 exit bar already carries 0.62 bp), but any cash-hours rule on an index is affected, and the
   in-sample years are charged unevenly.
2. **Bar-level spread at session switches.** The modelled spread of a bar comes from its closing quote, so
   the 17:30:00 open is charged the evening spread although Dukascopy's quote is still tight then
   (conservative by about 0.09 bp for exits at 17:30).
3. **M1 granularity.** `close_run_1725` depends on leaving at 17:30:00; the engine cannot say what a
   2-second delay costs. Exits at 17:29 and 17:31 are the bounds I can give (PF 1.13 and 1.17 in sample).
4. **Data hours change by year** (2015–2017 have no night session; 2013 is sparse), so nothing using the
   night session can be tested consistently on ger40.
5. `r2.null_test` groups bars by New York minute of day; for a European-clock rule the "same time of day"
   is one hour off during the 3–4 weeks a year when US and EU daylight saving differ. Minor.
6. `years_pos` counts 2013 (three months) as a year.
7. No engine bug found. Disclosure: `finalize.py` reads the stop distances of the 2024–2025 orders (asked
   for by the brief), and after the validation run I tabulated the modelled spread and the median price
   by year to 2025 for concern 1. No return of any 2022+ trade was computed outside `r2.validate`.
