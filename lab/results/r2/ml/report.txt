# Track `ml` — a learned filter on hourly breakouts ("our own indicator")

## Verdict

The learned filter works on one asset class only: **bitcoin and ether**. Both finalists pass every in-sample criterion and the single validation run, on both markets. I propose the filtered one.

- **F1, breakout + learned filter (proposed).** Validation 2022–2025: btcusd PF 1.33, 523 trades, +0.128 R per trade, 4/4 years; ethusd PF 1.22, 547 trades, +0.090 R per trade, 4/4 years.
- **F2, the same breakout without the filter (the baseline).** Validation: PF 1.14 on both markets, +0.053 and +0.054 R per trade. It passes, but its net edge is 2–3 bp per trade, about the size of its costs.
- **us100, ger40, xauusd: nothing survives.** The unfiltered breakout is break-even there and no model finds anything to learn (walk-forward PF 0.91–1.09, t below 1).

(R = result of a trade divided by the money risked to the stop. PF = gains / losses.)

Three warnings:

1. **The edge is shrinking.** btcusd went from +0.31 R per trade in-sample to +0.13 in validation; 2021 and 2025 were barely positive (+0.03 R per trade in 2021; +6.8 R for the whole of 2025).
2. **This is not "many small gains".** Half the trades lose a little; the top 5 % of trades bring about 92 % of the in-sample profit.
3. **It is slow and it is one bet.** About one trade per weekday for the two markets together, and their same-day results are 0.69 correlated. At 1 % risk per trade, the validation result is roughly +29 % a year for both sleeves together, nowhere near +50 % a month.

Trials: 85 logged (81 distinct configurations + 4 full re-evaluations), budget 120. Validation: one `r2.validate` call per finalist sleeve (4 calls, 12 log lines). Nothing was re-tuned.

## 1. What was built

**Candidate generator** (fixed before the first trial, identical on every market):

- H1 bars from bid prices. Only "session bars" are used: bars opening 02:00–15:00 New York, Monday–Friday (14 a day). Every look-back counts session bars only.
- Volatility unit A = average (high − low) of the last 70 session bars.
- At each hourly close from 03:00 to 15:00 New York: close above the highest high of the previous 14 session bars → long candidate; below the lowest low → short candidate.
- Market entry on the next minute, fixed stop, exit the same day. Nothing is held overnight, so there is no swap.

Why session bars only: the index data has no overnight quotes from March 2015 to March 2018 (and 2013 is half empty before 09:00 New York). 02:00–16:00 New York is the only window present in every year, so the same information is used throughout.

**Filter.** 13 scale-free inputs (distances divided by A and signed by the trade side) plus 3 time-of-day and 2 weekday dummies: breakout distance, size and body of the breakout bar, 4 / 14 / 70-bar returns, channel width, volatility ratio (5 sessions vs 20), position in the 20-session range, number of same-side and opposite breakouts in the last 14 bars, distance beyond the previous day's high or low, move since the previous close. Left out on purpose: trade side (it would learn "the market went up"), spread level, round numbers, result of the previous candidate.

**Training.** The label is the net R of every candidate taken on its own (engine, FP costs). One model is trained on the pooled candidates of us100 + ger40 + xauusd + btcusd, with one threshold for all markets. Walk-forward: for each year Y from 2016 to 2021, train on candidates decided before 1 January Y minus 5 days (maximum hold is 13 hours), predict year Y. Scaling and threshold come from the training fold only. All in-sample numbers of a filtered sleeve are these out-of-fold predictions, run through `r2.evaluate`.

The plan, including the model recipe and the threshold, was written to `plan_predeclared.txt` before the first trial.

## 4. The finalists

**Rule (both).** Session bars and candidates as in section 1. One position per symbol. Stop 2.0 × A from the fill, never moved; no target; exit at market at 15:55 New York the same day. Parameters: `{"stop_k": 2.0, "hold_h": 13.0}` plus the defaults n = 14, vol_n = 70.

**F1 adds:** trade only if score ≥ 0.02792, where score = 0.03253 + Σ coef × clip((x − mean) / std, ±4) + dummies. Full-precision constants and the definition of each input are in `models/F1_frozen_scorecard.json`; the EA text spec is `ea_spec_F1.txt`. In words, the scorecard prefers a close far beyond the channel and beyond yesterday's extreme, a strong body on a bar that is not outsized, a breakout that is not late in a run, and a morning rather than a US-afternoon entry.

## 5. Is the filter real?

- **Permutation test** (outcomes shuffled within market and year, whole walk-forward redone, 500 times). Gain of the selected candidates over all candidates: btcusd +0.106 R against shuffled 0.001 ± 0.031 (largest 0.087); ethusd +0.091 against 0.002 ± 0.024 (largest 0.070). Both real gains are above all 500 shuffles. On us100, ger40 and xauusd the real gain sits inside the shuffled distribution (62nd, 74th, 10th percentile): nothing was learned there.
- **Transfer.** ethusd was never used for training. The btcusd candidates of 2017 are scored by a model trained only on us100, ger40 and xauusd of 2013–2016, and still separate (+0.139 R passed vs +0.008 rejected).
- **Validation, each candidate taken on its own** (derived from the every-signal reports): btcusd passed +0.147 R vs rejected +0.024; ethusd passed +0.112 vs rejected +0.011. In-sample the same split was +0.276 vs +0.010 and +0.152 vs −0.060. The separation halved but did not vanish.
- **Over-fit gap.** Frozen model scored in-sample: btcusd PF 2.035 against 1.844 out-of-fold; ethusd 1.633 against 1.568.
- In total R, the filter helped on btcusd in validation (67.1 R vs 48.7 R with 43 % fewer trades) and was neutral on ethusd (49.0 R vs 50.2 R, with a smaller drawdown).

## 6. Robustness and execution (in-sample)

- **Causality.** Orders built on data cut at 4 random times equal the full-data orders up to those times, for 3 markets × 3 configurations (36 comparisons, all identical). An independent slow re-computation of 12 random candidates from raw M1 rows matches inputs, fill, stop and result exactly.
- **Entry delay** (F1): 1 minute late PF 1.778 btcusd / 1.554 ethusd; 5 minutes 1.809 / 1.507; 30 minutes 1.662 / 1.590. It degrades slowly and does not collapse.
- **Costs** (F1, spread and slippage multiplied): × 2 PF 1.761 / 1.450; × 3 1.629 / 1.322; × 5 1.474 / 1.124. In validation the margin is thinner: F1 ethusd nets 6 bp per trade against about 8–9 bp of costs, so it is the sleeve most exposed to a cost revision. F1 btcusd nets 14 bp against about 4 bp.
- **Random side at the rule's own entry times:** the rule is above all 200 replays on each sleeve.
- **Concentration** (F1 btcusd): top 1 % of trades = 41 % of the profit, top 5 % = 92 %; PF 1.50 without the top 1 %; median trade +0.02 R; 474 time exits, 197 stops; longest losing run 6; worst week −3.9 R. F1 ethusd: 30 %, 96 %, PF 1.40 without the top 1 %.
- **Data years with full coverage only** (2019–2021): F1 btcusd PF 1.684, ethusd 1.469.
- **What the trade really is.** 55 % of F1 btcusd trades and 53 % of F1 ethusd trades are entered at 03:00 New York, the first decision of the day. Because session bars skip the night, that decision compares the price with yesterday's whole 02:00–16:00 range. The sleeve is mostly "crypto opens the European morning outside yesterday's range → follow it until the New York close".

## 9. Caveats, data and engine concerns

- **Selection.** Crypto is one of four asset groups tried (six markets touched). The baseline effect is strong on its own (btcusd t 3.5, null 100th percentile), and the first pre-declared recipe was kept, so there was no selection among models.
- **Overlap with the crypto track.** F1 and F2 are almost certainly the same underlying effect as any bitcoin / ether breakout sleeve from the `crypto` track. Count them as one bet in the portfolio.
- **Short history.** btcusd in-sample is 2017-05 to 2021, ethusd 2018 to 2021. The "years positive" criterion rests on 4–5 years.
- **Early bitcoin data is thin.** 2017–2018 have 92 missing days and many short days (the `crypto` track's data check). `gl.exit_index` moves a time exit to the last bar before a data hole longer than 30 minutes, which an EA cannot know; it is not directional but it touches 2017–2018 results.
- **Costs are provisional** (`fp_costs.json`): constant 2.2 bp on btcusd and 6.5 bp on ethusd at all hours. FP Markets' real crypto spread, trading hours and 1:20 leverage could not be verified: the shared web-search budget was exhausted, so no source was consulted in this session.
- **Server clock.** The rule assumes FP server time = New York + 7 hours all year. Check it at install; otherwise compute New-York time in the EA.
- **`r2.null_test`** draws random days from all calendar days, week-ends included, while the rule trades Monday–Friday. The rule is still at the 100th percentile, and the random-side null at its own entry times agrees.
- **No basket helper in the toolkit.** I pooled the in-sample trades myself; my replica of the null reproduces `r2.null_test` exactly on each sleeve (100 / 100 / 100 / 99.5).
- **No engine bug found.** One behaviour to know: with one position at a time, an order activating on the bar where the previous trade exits is skipped.
- **Warm-up for the 2026 holdout.** Load M1 from 2025-11-01 and keep trades entered from 2026-01-01 (a frame starting 6 weeks earlier gives identical orders).
